How to take a full-page screenshot, lazy-loaded parts included

Updated October 17, 2026
On this page

    A browser can only photograph what's on screen. Full-page tools get around that by scrolling and stitching, and the hard part is everything that goes wrong along the way: images that haven't loaded yet, headers that follow you down the page, and pages too tall for one image.

    Capture the whole page

    1. Open the page and press ⌘ ⇧ E (Ctrl ⇧ E on Windows and Linux), or click the DivPic icon.
    2. Pick PNG in the toolbar, then click Full page.
    3. Leave the tab alone for a moment while DivPic scrolls through the page.
    4. The finished image is saved or copied, depending on your setting.

    What DivPic does behind the scenes

    ProblemWhat DivPic does
    Images that load as you scrollScrolls through the page first so lazy-loaded images and sections are there before capturing.
    Content below the foldPhotographs the page one screen at a time and stitches the pieces into a single image.
    Sticky headers and chat bubblesTakes each piece from the part of the screen they don't cover, so they don't repeat down the image.
    Panels that scroll on their ownScrolls the panel itself as well as the page, so its hidden content is included.
    DivPic's own toolbarKept out of the image.
    Elements work the same way. If you select a single element that's taller than your screen, DivPic scrolls and stitches just that element.

    Very long pages

    Browsers can't create an image taller than roughly 32,000 pixels. For pages longer than that, DivPic trims the bottom rather than giving you a blank or broken file.

    Tip: for an extremely long page:

    • Capture it in parts by selecting large sections as elements
    • Or use Figma mode, which reads the layout instead of taking pictures

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is the bottom of a very long page cut off?

    Browsers can't create images taller than about 32,000 pixels. For pages longer than that, DivPic trims the bottom instead of failing. Capture the lower part as a separate element.

    Does the page jump around while it captures?

    Yes, briefly. DivPic scrolls the page to photograph each part. Leave the tab alone until it finishes.

    Can I send a full page to Figma instead?

    Yes. Turn on Figma mode before clicking Full page. See copying a website into Figma.
